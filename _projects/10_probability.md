## A sequence that converges to its own target

Let $$Y$$ be exponential with rate 1 and let $$X_n$$ be uniform on $$(Y, Y + 1/n)$$ given $$Y$$. The joint density is $$n e^{-y}$$ on that strip. Given $$Y$$, the distance $$X_n - Y$$ is uniform on $$(0, 1/n)$$, so $$E\left[(X_n - Y)^2\right] = 1/(3n^2) \to 0$$: $$X_n$$ converges to $$Y$$ in mean square, hence in probability and in distribution. In probability it is explicit, $$P(\lvert X_n - Y\rvert > \varepsilon) = \max(0, 1 - n\varepsilon)$$, and a simulation traces the same curves: the probability stays near 1 until $$n$$ approaches $$1/\varepsilon$$ and then falls to zero.

## Credit ratings as a Markov chain

Standard and Poor's publishes each year the matrix of one-year transition probabilities between rating classes, from AAA to CCC/C, plus default (D) and not rated (NR). I took the 2023 matrices for the United States, the European Union and emerging and frontier markets and treated each as a time-homogeneous Markov chain: classified its states, then raised the matrix to a power to get multi-year probabilities. In every market D and NR are absorbing, and so is AAA in the US and European matrices, where no AAA issuer was downgraded in the sample.

The probability that a BBB-rated issuer defaults within three years is the (BBB, D) entry of the cube of the matrix:

| market                        | BBB default within 3 years |
| :---------------------------- | -------------------------: |
| United States                 |                      0.90% |
| European Union                |                      0.17% |
| Emerging and frontier markets |                      0.03% |

Starting from issuers spread uniformly over the nine states, after 10 years 28.3% of those in the US matrix sit at A or better and 22.1% have defaulted; after 30 years, 21.2% and 24.6%. For the European matrix the figures are 30.2% and 19.0% at 10 years, 23.1% and 19.8% at 30. The emerging-market matrix has no AAA issuers, so its AAA row is empty and I leave its long-horizon figures out.

## How many shuffles to move the bottom card to the top?

Take a deck of 52 cards and shuffle it by taking the top card and putting it back in a uniformly random position. How many shuffles until the card that started at the bottom reaches the top? The question asks for a simulation, and the code is short:

```python
def one_run(n=52):
    deck = list(range(n))                       # card n-1 starts at the bottom, card 0 on top
    count = 0
    while deck[0] != n - 1:                     # until the original bottom card is on top
        card = deck.pop(0)                      # take the top card ...
        deck.insert(random.randint(0, n - 1), card)   # ... and put it back anywhere, uniformly
        count += 1
    return count, deck.index(0)                 # shuffles needed, and where the original top card ended up
```

Over 10,000 runs the average is 235.9 shuffles, with a spread from 111 to 748.

The exact mean is $$52\,(1 + 1/2 + \dots + 1/51) = 235.0$$: the bottom card climbs one place whenever the inserted card lands below it, which happens with probability $$(b + 1)/52$$ when $$b$$ cards are below it, so it needs $$52/(b + 1)$$ shuffles on average for that step, for $$b$$ from 0 to 50, and the steps add up to the harmonic sum. The simulation agrees to within its standard error of 0.7. I also recorded where the card that started on top ends up at that moment: apart from position 0, taken by the bottom card, it is spread across the 51 other positions with no pattern that the simulation can detect (chi-square test against uniform, p = 0.78).
